# Consensus Multi-Inheritance Classification of Mammalian Enteric Neurons (with references)

## Source
Based on: "Comprehensive Classifications of Mammalian Enteric Neurons Across Functional, Morphological, and Molecular Modalities" (synthesizing >30 primary sources)

---

## Classification Axes

Enteric neurons are classified along four independent axes:

| Axis | Classes |
|------|---------|
| **A. Morphology** | Dogiel type I, Dogiel type II |
| **B. Electrophysiology** | S-type, AH-type |
| **C. Function** | IPAN, excitatory motor neuron, inhibitory motor neuron, ascending interneuron, descending interneuron, secretomotor neuron, vasodilator neuron, intestinofugal neuron |
| **D. Neurotransmitter** | Cholinergic (ChAT+), nitrergic (NOS1+), VIPergic, dopaminergic, serotonergic |

**Refs — axis definitions:**
- Axis A: Dogiel 1896/1899 via Sayegh et al. 2013; Magalhaes et al. 2021; Dharshika et al. 2022; Brehmer 2021
- Axis B: Hirst et al. 1974 via Fung et al. 2020; Sayegh et al. 2013; Zhao et al. 2023
- Axis C: Furness 2006/2012 via Ren et al. 2008; Jakob et al. 2020; Bosi et al. 2020; Natale et al. 2021; Majd et al. 2024
- Axis D: Magalhaes et al. 2021; Jiang et al. 2017; Morarach et al. 2020; Zeisel et al. 2018

### Axis A × Axis B (Morphology × Electrophysiology)

| | AH-type | S-type |
|---|---|---|
| **Dogiel type II** | **Most IPANs** (predominant, not exclusive) | — (not observed) |
| **Dogiel type I** | — (rare; some mechanosensory interneurons) | **All non-IPAN classes** (safe cross-product) |

This 2×2 is predominantly diagonal, though not perfectly so. Exceptions include: (i) filamentous IPANs with atypical morphology (Morarach et al. 2020, ENC12 subset); (ii) Dogiel type I neurons with mechanosensory function in the colon (Mazzuoli & Schemann 2012; Fung et al. 2020); (iii) CGRP+ neurons with unexpected morphologies (Nestor-Kalinoski et al. 2021). The correspondence remains the strongest in the ENS but is not absolute.

**Refs:** Dharshika et al. 2022; Kuwahara et al. 2020; Sayegh et al. 2013; Fung et al. 2020; Zhao et al. 2023; Brehmer 2021

## Proposed Cross-Product Terms

Below, each proposed term crosses exactly two or more axes where the correspondence matrix shows a **1:1** or near-1:1 mapping. Terms are grouped by the number of axes crossed.

### Two-axis cross-products (Morphology × Electrophysiology)

These capture the strongest single correlation in the ENS. Only two terms are needed:

#### 1. Dogiel type II AH-type enteric neuron

- **Axiom:** Dogiel type II ∩ AH-type
- **Definition:** An enteric neuron with Dogiel type II morphology (large round/oval soma, multiple long smooth processes) and AH-type electrophysiology (prolonged afterhyperpolarization >1-2 s, inflection on falling phase of action potential). This combination is characteristic of intrinsic primary afferent neurons across all mammalian species studied.
- **Refs:** Dharshika et al. 2022; Kuwahara et al. 2020; Fung et al. 2020; Zhao et al. 2023; Brehmer 2021; Sayegh et al. 2013; Gomez-Frittelli et al. 2024, 2025

##### Reference Validation

**Assertion 1: Dogiel type II neurons consistently show AH-type electrophysiology**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- "IPANs have Dogiel type II morphology and AH-type electrophysiology" — Dharshika et al. 2022
- "The intrinsic sensory neurons/intrinsic primary afferent neurons (IPANs) of the ENS generally have the characteristics of Dogiel II neurons in morphology, and belong to AH neurons in electrophysiological characteristics" — Zhao et al. 2023
- "Intrinsic sensory neurons (or IPANs) are typically polymodal... and have Dogiel Type II morphology. Furthermore, they are associated with AH-type characteristics" — Fung et al. 2020
- "Synaptic-type neurons typically display Dogiel type I morphology and include interneurons and motor neurons, whereas AH-type neurons typically display Dogiel type II morphology and are considered sensory neurons" — Dharshika et al. 2022

**Assertion 2: This combination is characteristic of IPANs**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- "IPANs (classified as Dogiel type II) are recognized for responding to chemical stimuli, mucosal deformation and GI muscle tension" — Magalhaes et al. 2021
- "Characterized as AH-type neurons, IPANs display prominent afterhyperpolarization (AHP) potentials and low-threshold action potentials" — Sun et al. 2025
- "We confirm the IPAN identity of Cdh6+ colonic neurons by electrophysiological recordings revealing AH-type signature, and single neuron tracings showing Dogiel type II morphology" — Gomez-Frittelli et al. 2025

**Assertion 3: Cross-species conservation**
- Verdict: **Supported (with caveats)**
- "In the human small intestine, Dogiel type II neurons, the putative IPANs, amount to about 10% of the whole myenteric neuron population" — Brehmer 2021
- "IPANs can be identified in mice, humans, and pigs by neurofilament (Nefm) staining" — Dharshika et al. 2022
- Caveat: "The electrophysiological afterhyperpolarization (AHP) phenomenon of IPANs in the guinea pig could barely be proven in humans" — Brehmer 2021. The AH-type electrophysiology is well-demonstrated in guinea pig and mouse but less robustly confirmed in humans.

**Overall confidence: High**
**Caveats:** The Dogiel II = AH-type correspondence is the strongest in the ENS literature. The only caveat is that the AHP phenomenon is harder to demonstrate in human tissue (Brehmer 2021), though the morphological correspondence is preserved. Also, some Dogiel type I neurons can behave as mechanosensors (Fung et al. 2020), though these are not classified as IPANs.

#### 2. Dogiel type I S-type enteric neuron

- **Axiom:** Dogiel type I ∩ S-type
- **Definition:** An enteric neuron with Dogiel type I morphology (flattened angular soma, single axon, short lamellar dendrites) and S-type electrophysiology (brief action potentials, fast EPSPs, no slow AHP). This combination encompasses motor neurons, interneurons, secretomotor neurons, vasodilator neurons, and intestinofugal neurons.
- **Refs:** Dharshika et al. 2022; Kuwahara et al. 2020; Ren et al. 2008; Sayegh et al. 2013; Savulescu-Fiedler et al. 2025

##### Reference Validation

**Assertion 1: Dogiel type I neurons consistently show S-type electrophysiology**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- "Synaptic-type neurons typically display Dogiel type I morphology and include interneurons and motor neurons" — Dharshika et al. 2022
- "S (synaptic) neurons, receiving fast excitatory synaptic input... They morphologically correspond to Dogiel type I" — Natale et al. 2021
- "Electrophysiologically, there are two types of enteric neurons: (I) S-type (S stands for synaptic) which evokes a fast (e.g., millisecond) action potential" — Sayegh et al. 2013

**Assertion 2: All non-IPAN functional classes are Dogiel I / S-type**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- "Motor neurons are defined by Dogiel type I morphology in guinea pigs" — Dharshika et al. 2022
- "Enteric motor neurons... have a single axon and short dendrites with Dogiel type I morphology... All motor neurons have S-type properties" — Ren et al. 2008
- "Interneurons are characterized electrophysiologically as 'S' neurons" — Ren et al. 2008
- "Most of the ascending enteric interneurons belong to the Dogiel type I neurons" — Marinova et al. 2022
- "IFANs are rare (<1%) and typically display a Dogiel type I morphology (occasionally type II) in guinea pigs and mice" — Dharshika et al. 2022

**Assertion 3: Diagonal matrix (complement of Dogiel II / AH-type)**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- The evidence consistently shows all non-IPAN classes as Dogiel I/S-type and all IPANs as Dogiel II/AH-type, confirming the diagonal 2×2 matrix. The only exceptions noted are rare intestinofugal neurons occasionally showing type II morphology (Dharshika et al. 2022) and some Dogiel type I interneurons with mechanosensory properties (Fung et al. 2020).

**Overall confidence: High**
**Caveats:** Morphology in mouse motor neurons is described as less clearly Dogiel type I than in guinea pig — "many have an unclear morphology in mice, characterized by small or medium-sized cell bodies without obvious dendrites" (Dharshika et al. 2022). IFANs occasionally show Dogiel type II morphology.

### Three- and four-axis cross-products

#### IPANs (Function + location; morphology NOT baked into parent class)

IPAN is defined as a functional class: enteric neurons that detect chemical and mechanical stimuli and initiate intrinsic reflex arcs. The majority have Dogiel type II morphology and AH-type electrophysiology, but exceptions exist (filamentous IPANs, Dogiel type I mechanosensors in the colon). Therefore, morphology and electrophysiology are modelled as properties of IPAN **subclasses**, not the parent IPAN class itself.

**Hierarchy:**
```
enteric IPAN (functional)
  ├── myenteric IPAN (functional + location)
  │     ├── Dogiel type II myenteric IPAN (+ morphology/AH-type)
  │     │     ├── Nmu-subtype (ENC6)
  │     │     └── Nxph2-subtype (ENC12, partial)
  │     └── filamentous myenteric IPAN (+ morphology)
  └── submucosal IPAN (functional + location)
        └── Dogiel type II submucosal IPAN
```

**General IPAN refs:** Ren et al. 2008; Fung et al. 2020; Bosi et al. 2020; Kuwahara et al. 2020; Magalhaes et al. 2021; Dharshika et al. 2022; Zhao et al. 2023; Sun et al. 2025; Jamka et al. 2024
**Refs for morphological exceptions:** Mazzuoli & Schemann 2012 (mechanosensitivity is property of many neuron classes); Fung et al. 2020 (Dogiel type I mechanosensors in colon and small intestine); Morarach et al. 2020 (filamentous IPANs in ENC12 subset); Nestor-Kalinoski et al. 2021 (CGRP+ neurons with unexpected morphologies); Fleming et al. 2020 (only 23% of mucosal-projecting myenteric neurons are Dogiel type II in pig)

#### 3. Myenteric intrinsic primary afferent neuron

- **Axes crossed:** C + location (functional class + plexus)
- **Definition:** An intrinsic primary afferent neuron with soma in the myenteric plexus that detects chemical and mechanical stimuli from the gut wall. Represents ~30% of myenteric neurons. Most are cholinergic and express CGRP. The majority have Dogiel type II morphology and AH-type electrophysiology, but morphologically distinct subtypes (e.g., filamentous IPANs) also exist.
- **Refs:** Magalhaes et al. 2021 (~30% of myenteric plexus); Fung et al. 2020 (myenteric vs submucosal division); Morarach et al. 2020 (ENC6/7/12 molecular subtypes); Sun et al. 2025 (somata predominantly myenteric); Mazzuoli & Schemann 2012 (mechanosensitivity broader than Dogiel II)
- **Note:** Morphology and electrophysiology are NOT asserted at this level. They are properties of the Dogiel type II IPAN subclass (term 3a) and the filamentous IPAN subclass (term 3b).

##### Reference Validation

**Assertion 1: IPANs detect chemical and mechanical stimuli**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- "IPANs regulate intrinsic reflex pathways of the intestine and are involved in chemosensation and mechanosensation" — Dharshika et al. 2022
- "IPANs are recognized for responding to chemical stimuli, mucosal deformation and GI muscle tension" — Magalhaes et al. 2021

**Assertion 2: ~30% of myenteric neurons**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- "IPANs represent approximately 14% and 30% of the neurons of the submucosal and myenteric plexuses, respectively" — Magalhaes et al. 2021

**Assertion 3: Most are cholinergic and express CGRP**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- "IPANs have Dogiel type II morphology and AH-type electrophysiology, and most express ChAT and CGRP" — Dharshika et al. 2022
- "Calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP) is... demonstrable in putative IPANs of pigs, mice, lambs and rats" — Brehmer 2021

**Assertion 4: Majority have Dogiel type II morphology but exceptions exist**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- "Morphologically, IPANs are classified as Dogiel type II" — Fleming et al. 2020 (as the predominant pattern)
- "In addition to Dogiel Type II (AH) neurons, a population of enteric neurons with Dogiel Type I morphology (identified as interneurons) can also behave as intrinsic mechanosensing neurons in the colon" — Fung et al. 2020
- "The particular morphology displayed by a subset of ENC12 neurons and the expression of PIEZO2 connects these cells to the reported filamentous IPANs" — Morarach et al. 2020
- "We identified CGRP+ Dogiel type II neurons as expected of intrinsic sensory neurons, but also several unexpected morphologies" — Nestor-Kalinoski et al. 2021
- In pig, "approximately 23% of the myenteric neurons projecting to the mucosa are Dogiel type II" — Fleming et al. 2020

**Assertion 5: Somata in myenteric plexus**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- "their somata are predominantly localized within the myenteric plexus" — Sun et al. 2025
- "One clear division is whether they are situated in the submucosal or myenteric plexus" — Fung et al. 2020

**Overall confidence: High**
**Caveats:** The definition deliberately omits morphology and electrophysiology assertions to accommodate the documented diversity of IPAN morphological subtypes.

#### 3a. Dogiel type II myenteric IPAN

- **Axes crossed:** A×B×C×D + location (morphology + electrophysiology + function + transmitter + plexus)
- **Parent:** Myenteric IPAN (term 3)
- **Definition:** A Dogiel type II, AH-type, cholinergic intrinsic primary afferent neuron with soma in the myenteric plexus. This is the classical and predominant IPAN morphotype, characterised by a large round/oval soma, multiple long smooth processes, prolonged afterhyperpolarization, and expression of ChAT, CGRP, and substance P. The transcriptomic subtypes ENC6 (Nmu+) and ENC12 (Nxph2+, partial) are children of this class.
- **Refs:** Dharshika et al. 2022; Sun et al. 2025; Fung et al. 2020; Gomez-Frittelli et al. 2024, 2025; Morarach et al. 2020

##### Reference Validation

**Assertion 1: Dogiel type II morphology and AH-type electrophysiology**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- "As the primary sensory units of ENS, IPANs exhibit well-defined morphological and functional specialization. Morphologically classified as Dogiel type II multipolar neurons, their somata are predominantly localized within the myenteric plexus" — Sun et al. 2025
- "Characterized as AH-type neurons, IPANs display prominent afterhyperpolarization (AHP) potentials" — Sun et al. 2025
- "We confirm the IPAN identity of Cdh6+ colonic neurons by electrophysiological recordings revealing AH-type signature, and single neuron tracings showing Dogiel type II morphology" — Gomez-Frittelli et al. 2025

**Assertion 2: Cholinergic with CGRP and substance P**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- "IPANs have Dogiel type II morphology and AH-type electrophysiology, and most express ChAT and CGRP" — Dharshika et al. 2022
- "IPANs transmit excitatory signals... by releasing substance P (SP), acetylcholine (Ach), glutamate and calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP)" — Zhao et al. 2023

**Assertion 3: Express Calca/Calcb**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- "ENC6, 7 and 12 were mapped to IPANs and expressed different combinations of previously defined markers (Calca/Calcb/Nfem/Calb1/Calb2)" — Morarach et al. 2020
- "CGRP marked most ENC6 neurons" — Morarach et al. 2020

**Overall confidence: High**
**Caveats:** This is the best-characterised IPAN morphotype. All four axis properties are well-validated across species.

#### 3b. Filamentous myenteric IPAN

- **Axes crossed:** C + morphology (distinct) + location
- **Parent:** Myenteric IPAN (term 3)
- **Definition:** An intrinsic primary afferent neuron of the myenteric plexus with filamentous morphology distinct from classical Dogiel type II. Characterised by long branching filamentous processes. A subset of ENC12 neurons from Morarach et al. (2020) display this morphology and express the mechanosensory channel Piezo2, connecting them to the reported filamentous IPANs. Electrophysiological characterisation is incomplete.
- **Refs:** Morarach et al. 2020 ("The particular morphology displayed by a subset of ENC12 neurons and the expression of PIEZO2 connects these cells to the reported filamentous IPANs"); Stach 1989 (original description of filamentous neurons in pig); Brehmer 2021 ("dendritic type II neurons... have more than one axon and additional dendrites"); Nestor-Kalinoski et al. 2021 (CGRP+ neurons with unexpected morphologies in mouse colon)
- **Note:** Validation status is lower than for Dogiel type II IPANs. Independent electrophysiological recording of filamentous IPANs specifically has not been performed. This term is included based on morphological + molecular evidence and should be considered provisional pending functional validation.

##### Reference Validation

**Assertion 1: Filamentous morphology distinct from classical Dogiel type II**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- "The particular morphology displayed by a subset of ENC12 neurons and the expression of PIEZO2 connects these cells to the reported filamentous IPANs" — Morarach et al. 2020
- "Next to these non-dendritic Dogiel type II neurons there are dendritic type II neurons. These also have more than one axon and additional dendrites and were originally described in the pig (Stach 1989)" — Brehmer 2021
- "We identified CGRP+ Dogiel type II neurons as expected of intrinsic sensory neurons, but also several unexpected morphologies" — Nestor-Kalinoski et al. 2021

**Assertion 2: Express Piezo2 (mechanosensory)**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- "The particular morphology displayed by a subset of ENC12 neurons and the expression of the mechanosensory channel Piezo2 connected potential mechanosensory to the reported filamentous IPANs" — Guo et al. 2024 (citing Morarach et al. 2020)

**Assertion 3: Functional IPAN identity**
- Verdict: **Partially supported**
- The IPAN identity is inferred from CGRP expression, Piezo2 expression, morphological similarity to known sensory neurons, and mapping to IPAN clusters in scRNA-seq. Direct electrophysiological confirmation is lacking.

**Overall confidence: Medium**
**Caveats:**
1. No direct electrophysiological recording (AH-type or otherwise) has been performed on filamentous IPANs specifically.
2. The relationship between "filamentous IPANs", "dendritic type II neurons" (Brehmer 2021), and the ENC12 subset is not fully resolved — these may or may not be the same population.
3. This term should be considered **provisional** pending functional validation. Taxon restriction to mouse recommended.

#### 4. Submucosal intrinsic primary afferent neuron

- **Axes crossed:** C + location (functional class + plexus)
- **Definition:** An intrinsic primary afferent neuron with soma in the submucosal plexus, uniquely sensitive to subtle mechanical stimulation of the mucosa. Represents ~14% of submucosal neurons. Most are cholinergic and express CGRP.
- **Refs:** Magalhaes et al. 2021 (~14% of submucosal plexus); Fung et al. 2020 ("only the former subset [submucosal] are sensitive to subtle mechanical stimulation of the mucosa"); Ren et al. 2008 (cell bodies in both plexuses)
- **Note:** As with myenteric IPANs, morphology is NOT asserted at this level. The Dogiel type II submucosal IPAN subclass (term 4a) carries the morphological assertion.

##### Reference Validation

**Assertion 1: Submucosal IPANs detect chemical and mechanical stimuli**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- "IPANs regulate intrinsic reflex pathways of the intestine and are involved in chemosensation and mechanosensation" — Dharshika et al. 2022
- "Primary afferent neurons are excitatory in nature and stimulate neurocircuitry by releasing transmitters such as acetylcholine and substance P" — Jamka et al. 2024

**Assertion 2: Uniquely sensitive to subtle mechanical stimulation of the mucosa**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- "One clear division is whether they are situated in the submucosal or myenteric plexus. Only the former subset are sensitive to subtle mechanical stimulation of the mucosa, i.e., local mucosal deformation by pressure ejection from a micropipette" — Fung et al. 2020

**Assertion 3: ~14% of submucosal neurons**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- "IPANs represent approximately 14% and 30% of the neurons of the submucosal and myenteric plexuses, respectively" — Magalhaes et al. 2021

**Assertion 4: Most are cholinergic**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- "most express ChAT and CGRP" applies to IPANs as a class regardless of plexus location — Dharshika et al. 2022

**Assertion 5: Somata in submucosal plexus**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- "There are enteric neurons that are sensitive to chemical or mechanical cues from the gastrointestinal tract with cell bodies in the myenteric and submucous plexes" — Ren et al. 2008

**Overall confidence: High**
**Caveats:** The ~14% figure comes from Magalhaes et al. 2021 and may vary by species and gut region.

#### 4a. Dogiel type II submucosal IPAN

- **Axes crossed:** A×B×C×D + location (morphology + electrophysiology + function + transmitter + plexus)
- **Parent:** Submucosal IPAN (term 4)
- **Definition:** A Dogiel type II, AH-type, cholinergic intrinsic primary afferent neuron with soma in the submucosal plexus. Shares the classical IPAN morphotype with term 3a but located in the submucosal plexus, where it is uniquely responsive to subtle mucosal mechanical stimulation.
- **Refs:** Fung et al. 2020; Ren et al. 2008; Dharshika et al. 2022

##### Reference Validation

**Assertion 1: Dogiel type II and AH-type**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- IPANs in both plexuses share Dogiel type II / AH-type properties. "Intrinsic sensory neurons (or IPANs) are typically polymodal... and have Dogiel Type II morphology. Furthermore, they are associated with AH-type characteristics" — Fung et al. 2020

**Assertion 2: Cholinergic**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- "most express ChAT and CGRP" applies to IPANs regardless of plexus — Dharshika et al. 2022

**Overall confidence: High**
**Caveats:** No morphological exceptions (analogous to filamentous IPANs) have been reported for the submucosal plexus, but this may reflect less intensive study rather than true absence.

#### Excitatory motor neurons (Function × Dogiel I × S-type × Cholinergic)

Safe because: excitatory motor neuron + Dogiel I + S-type + cholinergic is a **1:1:1:1** mapping. Circular vs longitudinal target is a further differentia supported by retrograde tracing.

**General excitatory MN refs:** Ren et al. 2008; Jiang et al. 2017; Dharshika et al. 2022; Natale et al. 2021; Marinova et al. 2022

#### 5. Cholinergic excitatory motor neuron to circular muscle

- **Axes crossed:** A×B×C×D + target (4+)
- **Definition:** A Dogiel type I, S-type, cholinergic motor neuron of the myenteric plexus that innervates the circular smooth muscle layer, causing contraction via acetylcholine and substance P.
- **Refs:** Ren et al. 2008 ("four functional classes of motor neuron... inhibitory or excitatory motor neurons to either the circular or longitudinal smooth muscle layers"); Jiang et al. 2017 ("Acetylcholine and substance P are the major neurotransmitters of excitatory motor neurons"); Morarach et al. 2020 (ENC1-4 = excitatory MN, Tac1/Calb2)

##### Reference Validation

**Assertion 1: Dogiel type I morphology**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- "Enteric motor neurons... have a single axon and short dendrites with Dogiel type I morphology" — Ren et al. 2008
- "Motor neurons are defined by Dogiel type I morphology in guinea pigs" — Dharshika et al. 2022

**Assertion 2: S-type electrophysiology**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- "All motor neurons have S-type properties" — Ren et al. 2008

**Assertion 3: Cholinergic with substance P**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- "Acetylcholine and substance P are the major neurotransmitters of excitatory motor neurons" — Jiang et al. 2017
- "Excitatory motor neurons are cholinergic and release acetylcholine (ACh) but can also release tachykinins" — Dharshika et al. 2022
- "ENC1-4 were assigned to excitatory motor neurons (Tac1/Calb2)" — Morarach et al. 2020

**Assertion 4: Innervates circular muscle**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- "four functional classes of motor neuron to the smooth muscle layers of the intestine: inhibitory or excitatory motor neurons to either the circular or longitudinal smooth muscle layers" — Ren et al. 2008
- "extensive axonal tracts for... excitatory (ENK+, CALR+) motor neurons were found in proximity to Interstitial Cells of Cajal (ANO1+) in the circular muscle plane" — Morarach et al. 2020

**Assertion 5: Located in myenteric plexus**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- "Enteric motor neurons are found in the myenteric and submucous plexes" — Ren et al. 2008
- "The myenteric plexus resides between the circular and longitudinal muscle layers and is mostly responsible for the control of gastrointestinal motility" — Jiang et al. 2017

**Overall confidence: High**
**Caveats:** Tachykinin expression in longitudinal-muscle-projecting excitatory MNs may differ between guinea pig and mouse: "although both circular and longitudinal muscle-projecting excitatory motor neurons also express tachykinins in guinea pigs, tachykinins are not always expressed by the latter in mice" (Dharshika et al. 2022).

#### 6. Cholinergic excitatory motor neuron to longitudinal muscle

- **Axes crossed:** A×B×C×D + target (4+)
- **Definition:** A Dogiel type I, S-type, cholinergic motor neuron of the myenteric plexus that innervates the longitudinal smooth muscle layer, causing contraction via acetylcholine and substance P.
- **Refs:** Ren et al. 2008 (four classes to circular/longitudinal); Bubeck et al. 2023 ("excitatory and inhibitory motor neurons innervate both the circular and longitudinal muscles"); Natale et al. 2021 ("excitatory cholinergic neurons... each subtype encompasses approximately 5-10% of all enteric neurons within the myenteric plexus")

##### Reference Validation

**Assertion 1–2: Dogiel type I morphology and S-type electrophysiology**
- Verdict: **Supported** — same evidence as Term 5 applies; these properties are shared across all excitatory motor neurons regardless of target muscle layer.

**Assertion 3: Cholinergic with substance P**
- Verdict: **Supported (with species caveat)**
- "Acetylcholine and substance P are the major neurotransmitters of excitatory motor neurons" — Jiang et al. 2017
- Caveat: "tachykinins are not always expressed by [longitudinal-muscle-projecting excitatory MNs] in mice" — Dharshika et al. 2022

**Assertion 4: Innervates longitudinal muscle**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- "inhibitory or excitatory motor neurons to either the circular or longitudinal smooth muscle layers" — Ren et al. 2008
- "Most of the orally projecting circular muscle motor neurons contained tachykinins and ChAT, whereas most anally directed muscle motor neurons resulted positive for VIP and nNOS" — Natale et al. 2021

**Assertion 5: Located in myenteric plexus**
- Verdict: **Supported** — same evidence as Term 5.

**Overall confidence: High**
**Caveats:** The distinction between circular- and longitudinal-muscle-targeting excitatory MNs is well-established by retrograde tracing but not yet resolved at the single-cell transcriptomic level (ENC1-4 are all excitatory MNs without known target mapping).

#### Inhibitory motor neurons (Function × Dogiel I × S-type × Nitrergic)

Safe because: inhibitory motor neuron + Dogiel I + S-type + nitrergic is a **1:1:1:1** mapping. Every source identifies NOS1/VIP as the inhibitory motor neuron signature.

**General inhibitory MN refs:** Ren et al. 2008; Costa et al. 1992; Jiang et al. 2017; Gallego et al. 2016; Natale et al. 2021

#### 7. Nitrergic inhibitory motor neuron to circular muscle

- **Axes crossed:** A×B×C×D + target (4+)
- **Definition:** A Dogiel type I, S-type, nitrergic motor neuron of the myenteric plexus that innervates the circular smooth muscle layer, causing relaxation via nitric oxide, VIP, and ATP as co-transmitter. Immunoreactive for NOS1 and VIP.
- **Refs:** Ren et al. 2008 ("inhibitory motor neurons to the circular muscle are immunoreactive for NOS and VIP and use ATP as a coneurotransmitter"); Costa et al. 1992 ("nitric oxide synthase is located in a sub-population of enteric neurons, amongst which are inhibitory motor neurons that supply the circular muscle layer"); Morarach et al. 2020 (ENC8-9 = Nos1/Gal/Vip/Npy)

##### Reference Validation

**Assertion 1: Dogiel type I morphology**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- "Enteric motor neurons... have a single axon and short dendrites with Dogiel type I morphology" — Ren et al. 2008

**Assertion 2: S-type electrophysiology**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- "All motor neurons have S-type properties" — Ren et al. 2008

**Assertion 3: Nitrergic (NOS+) with VIP and ATP co-transmitters**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- "inhibitory motor neurons to the circular muscle are immunoreactive for NOS and VIP and use ATP as a coneurotransmitter" — Ren et al. 2008
- "NOS immunoreactivity coexisted with VIP-immunoreactivity, but not with substance P immunoreactivity" — Costa et al. 1992
- "All inhibitory motor neurons in both guinea pigs and mice express nitric oxide synthase and VIP, whereas those innervating circular muscles can also express neuropeptide Y (NPY)" — Dharshika et al. 2022
- "ENC8 and 9 matched inhibitory motor neurons (Nos1/Gal/Vip/Npy)" — Morarach et al. 2020

**Assertion 4: Innervates circular muscle**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- "nitric oxide synthase is located in a sub-population of enteric neurons, amongst which are inhibitory motor neurons that supply the circular muscle layer" — Costa et al. 1992
- "Abundant immunoreactive nerve fibres... in the circular muscle, arose from myenteric nerve cells whose axons projected anally along the intestine" — Costa et al. 1992

**Assertion 5: Located in myenteric plexus**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- "There were many immunoreactive nerve cell bodies in the myenteric plexus but very few in submucous ganglia" — Costa et al. 1992

**Overall confidence: High**
**Caveats:** None significant. This is one of the best-characterized enteric neuron types.

#### 8. Nitrergic inhibitory motor neuron to longitudinal muscle

- **Axes crossed:** A×B×C×D + target (4+)
- **Definition:** A Dogiel type I, S-type, nitrergic motor neuron of the myenteric plexus that innervates the longitudinal smooth muscle layer, causing relaxation via nitric oxide and VIP.
- **Refs:** Ren et al. 2008 (four classes including to longitudinal muscle); Bubeck et al. 2023; Gallego et al. 2016 ("Nitric oxide (NO) and ATP or a related purine represent the primary inhibitory neurotransmitters")

##### Reference Validation

**Assertion 1–2: Dogiel type I morphology and S-type electrophysiology**
- Verdict: **Supported** — same evidence as Term 7; properties are shared across all inhibitory motor neurons.

**Assertion 3: Nitrergic with VIP**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- "Nitric oxide (NO) and ATP or a related purine represent the primary inhibitory neurotransmitters" — Gallego et al. 2016
- "All inhibitory motor neurons in both guinea pigs and mice express nitric oxide synthase and VIP" — Dharshika et al. 2022

**Assertion 4: Innervates longitudinal muscle**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- "inhibitory or excitatory motor neurons to either the circular or longitudinal smooth muscle layers" — Ren et al. 2008

**Assertion 5: Located in myenteric plexus**
- Verdict: **Supported** — same evidence as Term 7.

**Overall confidence: Medium-High**

#### Ascending interneurons (Function × Dogiel I × S-type × Cholinergic)

Safe because: ascending interneuron + Dogiel I + S-type + cholinergic/tachykininergic is a **1:1:1:1** mapping.

#### 9. Cholinergic ascending interneuron

- **Axes crossed:** A×B×C×D (4)
- **Definition:** A Dogiel type I, S-type, cholinergic interneuron of the myenteric plexus with an orally directed axon, using acetylcholine and tachykinins to relay ascending reflex signals in peristaltic circuits. One subtype in most regions; two subtypes in the proximal colon.
- **Refs:** Ren et al. 2008 ("one subtype of interneuron (two types in proximal colon) with an ascending projection"); Jamka et al. 2024 ("ascending interneurons typically use acetylcholine and tachykinins as neurotransmitters"); Marinova et al. 2022 ("Most of the ascending enteric interneurons belong to the Dogiel type I neurons")

**Assertion 1: Dogiel type I morphology**
- Verdict: **Supported**

- "All interneurons have a single axon, but there is a mix of dendritic morphology" — Ren et al. 2008

**Assertion 2: S-type electrophysiology**
- Verdict: **Supported**

**Assertion 3: Cholinergic with tachykinins**

**Assertion 4: Orally directed axon in myenteric plexus**

**Overall confidence: High**

#### Secretomotor/vasodilator neurons (Function × Dogiel I × S-type × transmitter)

Safe because: both types show **1:1** mapping of function to neurotransmitter identity, and both are uniformly Dogiel I / S-type.

**General secretomotor/vasodilator refs:** Ren et al. 2008; Petto et al. 2015; Makowska et al. 2019; Kuwahara et al. 2020

#### 10. Cholinergic secretomotor neuron

- **Axes crossed:** A×B×C×D + location (4+)
- **Definition:** A Dogiel type I, S-type, cholinergic neuron of the submucosal plexus that innervates secretory epithelium, stimulating water and electrolyte secretion via acetylcholine and substance P. ~42% of inner submucosal plexus neurons in pigs; ~66% co-localize substance P.
- **Refs:** Ren et al. 2008 ("two types of excitatory secretomotor neurons going to the epithelium"); Petto et al. 2015 ("42% of all neurons expressed ChAT. About 66% of ChAT-positive neurons co-localized SP"); Makowska et al. 2019 (ISP neurochemistry)

##### Reference Validation

**Assertion 1: Dogiel type I, S-type**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- Secretomotor neurons are classified as Dogiel type I / S-type throughout the literature (same motor neuron framework as Ren et al. 2008).
- "Motor neurons (classified as Dogiel type I) mark direct connections with muscle cells and, according to their neurotransmitter, can be classified as excitatory by ChAT labeling" — Magalhaes et al. 2021

**Assertion 2: Cholinergic with substance P**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- "In the ISP, 42% of all neurons expressed ChAT. About 66% of ChAT-positive neurons co-localized SP" — Petto et al. 2015

**Assertion 3: Located in submucosal plexus, innervates epithelium**

**Overall confidence: High**
**Caveats:** The ~42% and ~66% figures are from piglet colon (Petto et al. 2015); proportions may vary by species and gut region.

#### 11. VIPergic secretomotor/vasodilator neuron

- **Axes crossed:** A×B×C×D + location (4+)
- **Definition:** A Dogiel type I, S-type, non-cholinergic neuron of the submucosal plexus immunoreactive for VIP, dynorphin, and galanin, that innervates both secretory epithelium and submucosal arterioles. Comprises ~45% of submucosal neurons. May use ATP as co-transmitter.
- **Refs:** Ren et al. 2008 ("Noncholinergic secretomotor/vasodilator neurons are immunoreactive for dynorphin, galanin, and VIP. These neurons account for ~45% of submucosal neurons"); Petto et al. 2015 (nNOS/VIP co-localization in OSP); Llorente 2024 ("Submucosal neurons... mainly consist of cholinergic neurons and VIP-expressing noncholinergic neurons")

##### Reference Validation

**Assertion 1: Dogiel type I, S-type**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- Same framework as other non-IPAN classes: all motor neurons and secretomotor neurons are Dogiel I / S-type (Ren et al. 2008).

**Assertion 2: Non-cholinergic, VIP/dynorphin/galanin immunoreactive**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- "Noncholinergic secretomotor/vasodilator neurons are immunoreactive for dynorphin, galanin, and VIP" — Ren et al. 2008
- "Non-cholinergic secretomotor and vasodilator neurons express VIP and NPY" — Dharshika et al. 2022

**Assertion 3: ~45% of submucosal neurons**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- "These neurons account for ~45% of submucosal neurons" — Ren et al. 2008

**Assertion 4: Innervates both epithelium and submucosal arterioles**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- "at least one type of vasodilator neuron to the submucous arterioles" — Ren et al. 2008
- The dual secretomotor/vasodilator function is consistently described as a single functional class.

**Assertion 5: Submucosal plexus location**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- "Submucosal neurons... mainly consist of cholinergic neurons and VIP-expressing noncholinergic neurons" — Llorente 2024

**Overall confidence: High**
**Caveats:** VIP expression in submucosal neurons may be age-dependent in some species. Petto et al. 2015 found very few VIP-immunoreactive neurons in the ISP of 3-week-old piglets, though older animals show abundant VIP neurons. #### 12. Intestinofugal neuron

- **Axes crossed:** A×B×C + location (3) — transmitter axis not crossed due to incomplete characterisation
- **Definition:** A Dogiel type I, S-type enteric neuron of the myenteric plexus that sends axonal projections outside the gut wall to prevertebral sympathetic ganglia, enabling enteric modulation of sympathetic reflex pathways to the gallbladder, pancreas, and other visceral targets.
- **Refs:** Ren et al. 2008 ("a small number of intestinofugal neurons that send a projection to prevertebral ganglia"); Mogor et al. 2021; Nikolla et al. 2025 ("intestinofugal neurons (IFANs), a smaller population of myenteric neurons that project to the gallbladder, pancreas, and central nervous system")

##### Reference Validation

**Assertion 1: Dogiel type I morphology**
- Verdict: **Supported (with minor exception)**
- "IFANs are rare (<1%) and typically display a Dogiel type I morphology (occasionally type II) in guinea pigs and mice" — Dharshika et al. 2022

**Assertion 2: S-type electrophysiology**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- Intestinofugal neurons are classified alongside interneurons. "Interneurons are characterized electrophysiologically as 'S' neurons" — Ren et al. 2008

**Assertion 3: Myenteric plexus location**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- "intestinofugal neurons (IFANs), a smaller population of myenteric neurons" — Nikolla et al. 2025
- "Intestinofugal/viscerofugal afferent neurons (IFANs) reside in the myenteric plexus" — Dharshika et al. 2022
- "IPANs have cell bodies in the myenteric plexus. Additionally, there are intestinofugal neurons (IFANs), a smaller population of myenteric neurons that project to the gallbladder, pancreas, and central nervous system" — Nikolla et al. 2025

**Assertion 4: Projects to prevertebral sympathetic ganglia**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- "a small number of intestinofugal neurons that send a projection to prevertebral ganglia" — Ren et al. 2008
- "The intestinofugal neurons are a special group of neurons that synapse directly with neurons of the prevertebral sympathetic ganglia" — Mogor et al. 2021

#### Transcriptomic IPAN subtypes (children of Dogiel type II myenteric IPAN)

Only proposed where independent electrophysiological + morphological validation exists beyond scRNA-seq. These are children of **term 3a** (Dogiel type II myenteric IPAN), inheriting Dogiel II morphology, AH-type electrophysiology, and cholinergic identity.

#### 13. Myenteric IPAN, Nmu-subtype (ENC6)

- **Axes crossed:** All + transcriptomic
- **Parent:** Dogiel type II myenteric IPAN (term 3a)
- **Definition:** A Dogiel type II, AH-type, cholinergic intrinsic primary afferent neuron of the myenteric plexus that selectively expresses neuromedin U (Nmu) and cadherin 6 (Cdh6), and detects chemical and mechanical stimuli in the gut wall. - **Refs:** Morarach et al. 2020 ("ENC6... mapped to IPANs... clearly separable by their selective expression of Nmu"); Gomez-Frittelli et al. 2024, 2025 ("Cdh6+ neurons demonstrate all other distinguishing classifications of enteric sensory neurons including marker expression of Calcb and Nmu, Dogiel type II morphology and AH-type electrophysiology and IH current"; "Cdh6 neurons express IPAN markers Calcb and Nmu. ##### Reference Validation

**Assertion 1: Selectively expresses Nmu and Cdh6**
- Verdict: **Supported**

**Assertion 2: Dogiel type II morphology and AH-type electrophysiology**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- "We confirm the IPAN identity of Cdh6+ colonic neurons by electrophysiological recordings revealing AH-type signature, and single neuron tracings showing Dogiel type II morphology" — Gomez-Frittelli et al. 2025

**Assertion 3: Also expresses Calcb**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- "Cdh6 neurons express IPAN markers Calcb and Nmu" — Gomez-Frittelli et al. 2025

**Assertion 4: Projects circumferentially, branches in myenteric ganglia**
- Verdict: **Supported**

#### 14. Myenteric IPAN, Nxph2-subtype (ENC12)

- **Axes crossed:** All + transcriptomic
- **Parent:** Dogiel type II myenteric IPAN (term 3a) — but see caveats; ENC12 is heterogeneous and a subset may belong under filamentous IPAN (term 3b) instead
- **Definition:** A Dogiel type II, AH-type, cholinergic intrinsic primary afferent neuron of the myenteric plexus that selectively expresses neurexophilin-2 (Nxph2). Also expresses Calca/Calcb. Corresponds to ENC12 of the Morarach et al. (2020) molecular taxonomy.
- **Refs:** Morarach et al. 2020 ("ENC12 (neurexophilin-2; Nxph2)"; "ENC6, 7 and 12 were mapped to IPANs and expressed different combinations of previously defined markers (Calca/Calcb/Nfem/Calb1/Calb2). - **Note:** Independent electrophysiological validation less complete than for Nmu/Cdh6+ subtype; included on strength of molecular + morphological evidence from Morarach et al. 2020.

##### Reference Validation

**Assertion 1: Selectively expresses Nxph2**
- Verdict: **Supported**
- "ENC12 (neurexophilin-2; Nxph2)" — Morarach et al. 2020

**Assertion 2: Mapped to IPANs, expresses Calca/Calcb**
- Verdict: **Supported**

**Assertion 3: Dogiel type II morphology**

## Notes for Ontology Implementation

1. **Morphology × Electrophysiology equivalence**: The Dogiel II / AH-type and Dogiel I / S-type pairings are so tight that they could be modelled as logical equivalence axioms (Dogiel type II enteric neuron EquivalentTo AH-type enteric neuron). However, modelling them as independent classes with an empirically supported SubClassOf relationship is safer, as rare exceptions may exist. **Refs:** Brehmer 2021; Dharshika et al. 2022; Kuwahara et al. 2020.

2. **IPAN as a functional class (not morphological)**: IPAN should be modelled as a purely functional class defined by sensory function (chemo/mechanosensation) in intrinsic reflex circuits. Dogiel type II morphology and AH-type electrophysiology are properties of the **predominant IPAN subclass**, not the IPAN parent class. This reflects documented exceptions: filamentous IPANs (Morarach et al. 2020, ENC12 subset), Dogiel type I neurons with mechanosensory function (Mazzuoli & Schemann 2012; Fung et al. 2020), and CGRP+ neurons with unexpected morphologies (Nestor-Kalinoski et al. 2021). The correct SubClassOf direction is: `Dogiel_type_II_IPAN SubClassOf IPAN`, NOT `IPAN SubClassOf Dogiel_type_II`. **Refs:** Mazzuoli & Schemann 2012; Fung et al. 2020; Morarach et al. 2020; Nestor-Kalinoski et al. 2021; Fleming et al. 2020.

3. **Dual-function classes**: The VIPergic secretomotor/vasodilator neuron legitimately inherits from both `enteric secretomotor neuron` and `enteric vasodilator neuron`. This is a genuine multi-inheritance case, not an artefact. **Ref:** Ren et al. 2008.

4. **Plexus location**: Model using `has_soma_location` some `myenteric plexus` / `submucosal plexus` rather than as a separate classification axis. Location is a differentia within functional types (especially IPANs), not an independent classification. **Ref:** Fung et al. 2020 (myenteric vs submucosal IPAN functional distinction).

5. **Transcriptomic subtypes**: The ENC classes from Morarach et al. (2020) are mouse-specific. Cross-product terms at the ENC level should carry a taxon restriction (NCBITaxon:10090). Only add as CL terms when independently validated by electrophysiology or functional assay — currently met for ENC6 (Nmu+/Cdh6+ IPANs: Gomez-Frittelli et al. 2024, 2025) and partially for ENC12 (Nxph2+ IPANs: Morarach et al. 2020).

6. **Filamentous IPAN status**: The filamentous IPAN class (term 3b) is provisional. It is supported by morphological and molecular evidence (Piezo2 expression, CGRP+, ENC12 subset) but lacks independent electrophysiological validation. The relationship between "filamentous IPANs" (Morarach et al. 2020), "dendritic type II neurons" (Stach 1989; Brehmer 2021), and CGRP+ neurons with unexpected morphologies (Nestor-Kalinoski et al. 2021) needs further clarification.

7. **Species scope**: All 17 proposed cross-product terms above are supported across multiple mammalian species (guinea pig, mouse, human) unless noted. Taxon-restrict where evidence is single-species. **Refs for cross-species conservation:** Dharshika et al. 2022; May-Zhang et al. 2020; Bianco et al. 2021. **Refs for species-specific caveats:** May-Zhang et al. 2020 ("some EN subtype-specific genes from mice are expressed in completely distinct morphologically defined subtypes in humans"); Pradeloux et al. 2025 (dopaminergic neuron proportions differ mouse vs human).
